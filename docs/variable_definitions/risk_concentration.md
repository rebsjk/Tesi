# risk_share_top5, risk_share_top7, risk_share_top10, n_eff_risk, risk_entropy

Status: **implemented (2026-08-24)**, collinearity-checked, **not yet
integrated into the composite CSI** — that decision is explicitly deferred
until reviewed with the user/advisor (see "Composite-integration decision"
at the end). Estimation design (window, subset, shrinkage) is documented
separately in
`docs/methodology_notes/risk_concentration_covariance_estimation.md`; this
file covers the measures themselves.

## What these measure

All five are derived from the Euler (marginal) decomposition of portfolio
variance over the point-in-time top-100 subset (`N_SUBSET = 100`,
`src/concentration/covariance_estimation.py`):

```
p_i,t = w_i,t * (Sigma_t w_t)_i / (w_t' Sigma_t w_t),      sum_i p_i,t = 1
```

where `w_t` is each included name's ACTUAL point-in-time market weight
(not renormalized to sum to 1 over the subset — the identity above holds
regardless of scale, by Euler's theorem) and `Sigma_t` is the
Ledoit-Wolf-shrunk covariance matrix over the trailing 252-trading-day
window (see the estimation note). `p_i,t` **can be negative** — a name
whose covariance with the subset is negative despite a positive weight
contributes negatively to total variance. This is a known feature of
Euler decomposition, not a bug (see "Negative risk contributions" below).

- **`risk_share_top{5,7,10}`** = `sum_{i in topk-by-WEIGHT} p_i,t` — the
  fraction of the top-100 subset's total variance generated by the same
  named cohort CR-5/7/10 uses (top-k **by weight**, monthly-fixed,
  anchored at month-start — the identical global convention in
  `csi_construction.md`). Ranking by weight rather than by risk
  contribution is deliberate: ranking by risk contribution would make
  "this cohort's risk share" tautologically near 100% by construction.
  Direction: ↑ = more concentrated.
- **`n_eff_risk`** = `1 / sum_i p_i,t^2`, over the full 100-name subset —
  risk-space analogue of `effective_n`. Direction: ↓ = more concentrated
  (**inverted**, same convention as `effective_n`).
- **`risk_entropy`** = `1 - H(p_i,t^+) / ln(n)`, where `p_i,t^+ = max(p_i,t, 0)`
  (see "Negative risk contributions") and `n` = number of names actually
  included in Sigma_t that month. Direction: ↑ = more concentrated, same
  convention as `entropy_concentration`.

**Not part of `csi_construction.md`'s original spec:** `risk_entropy` was
added by analogy to the Phase-2 capital-concentration result, where
`entropy_concentration` turned out to be the one measure carrying
short-run information the `cr_k` family didn't. The internal collinearity
check below tests whether the same holds in risk-space.

## Concept boundary

None of these use the **correlation** matrix or its eigenvalues — that is
dependence concentration's territory (a separate, not-yet-built CSI
dimension), kept deliberately out of scope here even though it will reuse
the same `Sigma_t` estimation module.

## Negative risk contributions

**Status: convention decided (2026-08-24, after empirical review) —
clip-to-zero for `risk_entropy` only.** The paragraph below originally
(same date, first pass) characterized the pattern as "a rising cluster
from 2024-06 through 2026-07" — **corrected here**, on re-verification
against the full per-name-per-month distribution (not just the monthly
counts already in the panel output): the pattern is **episodic, not a
monotonic trend.

**Full distribution** (recomputed directly from `p_i,t` for all 308
months and all included names, not just the monthly counts in the output
CSV): `p_i,t < 0` occurred in **98 of ~30,000 name-month observations
(0.3%)**, across **13 of 308 months**. Magnitude: median **-0.00049**
(0.05% of total subset variance), 99th percentile **-0.0034**, sample
minimum **-0.0050** (2026-07). In the worst months (2026-05/06/07), the
single most negative contributor reaches a magnitude comparable to a
*median positive* contributor that month (ratio 1.06-1.17x) — not
negligible in those specific months, though still a small minority of
included names even then (19 of 96 in the worst case, 2026-07).

**Timing, corrected:** NOT a steady rise from mid-2024. The 13 affected
months are: 2001-04 (1 isolated event), then a sporadic handful in 2024
(2024-06, 09, 10, 11, 12: 0-6 events each), then a full **12 consecutive
months with zero negative contributions (2025-04 through 2026-03)**, then
a sharp reappearance and escalation in the final 4 months of the sample
(2026-04: 10, 2026-05: 15, 2026-06: 16, 2026-07: 19 of ~96-99 included
names). The correct characterization is a quiet base rate punctuated by
an acute recent episode, not a monotonic trend — the original wording is
corrected here specifically because it was checked against the full
distribution, not assumed from the monthly summary counts alone. Full
month-by-month counts in
`outputs/logs/build_risk_concentration_panel_20260824.txt`; full
per-name magnitudes in the ad-hoc diagnostic re-run 2026-08-24 (not
persisted to `data_final/`, reproducible from
`covariance_estimation.estimate_sigma_t` + `risk_measures.risk_contributions`
on the dates above).

### n_eff_risk is NOT affected by this convention

Recorded explicitly to prevent future ambiguity: `n_eff_risk = 1/sum(p_i,t^2)`
squares each contribution, which **erases sign entirely** — clipping
negatives to 0 versus leaving them signed versus taking `abs(p_i,t)` all
produce numerically **identical** `n_eff_risk` (verified to 4 decimal
places across all tested months). Only an exclude-and-renormalize
treatment (below) would change `n_eff_risk`, because renormalizing alters
the remaining shares' values, not just their sign. The negative-
contribution convention decided in this section governs **`risk_entropy`
only**.

### Convention comparison (quantified 2026-08-24)

Three candidate conventions for `risk_entropy`, compared on the months
where the effect is largest (recomputed with the existing, unmodified
`risk_measures`/`covariance_estimation` functions — no production code
changed to produce this comparison):

| Month | n negative | (a) clip-to-zero [adopted] | (b) abs value, raw | (b') abs value, renormalized | (c) exclude+renorm, n=original | (c) exclude+renorm, n=reduced |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-02 | 7  | 0.2884 | 0.2846 | 0.2868 | 0.2895 | 0.2775 |
| 2025-03 | 7  | 0.2719 | 0.2681 | 0.2704 | 0.2731 | 0.2611 |
| 2026-04 | 10 | 0.2627 | 0.2540 | 0.2597 | 0.2655 | 0.2481 |
| 2026-05 | 15 | 0.2641 | 0.2485 | 0.2595 | 0.2695 | 0.2417 |
| 2026-06 | 16 | 0.2557 | 0.2335 | 0.2496 | 0.2636 | 0.2334 |
| 2026-07 | 19 | 0.2587 | 0.2267 | 0.2507 | 0.2703 | 0.2332 |

Spread across conventions is **~4% relative in typical affected months**
(e.g. 2025-02: 0.2775-0.2895), widening to **~15% relative in the most
recent, most-affected months** (2026-07: 0.2267-0.2703). This is a real,
non-trivial sensitivity concentrated in exactly the part of the sample
most likely to matter for any near-term interpretation.

**Decision: clip-to-zero, denominator `ln(n)` on the original subset
size — option (a).** Implemented 2026-08-24; confirmed after empirical
review 2026-08-25 (the comparison table above, and the note that this
convention affects `risk_entropy` only, were both requested before
approval — see `docs/methodology_notes/risk_concentration_covariance_estimation.md`
for the analogous review discipline applied to the share-class decision
below).

- **Rejects (b) absolute value on economic grounds, not just numerically:**
  a name with a negative Euler contribution is *offsetting* portfolio
  variance, not generating it — treating `|large negative|` the same as a
  large positive contributor conflates "hedges the concentrated risk" with
  "is the concentrated risk," the opposite of what a concentration measure
  should distinguish. This holds regardless of the numbers above.
- **Rejects (c) exclude+renormalize on design-completeness grounds:** it
  introduces its own unresolved sub-choice (which `n` to use for `ln(n)`) —
  using the original `n` caps the achievable entropy below 1.0 in any
  month with negatives (an interpretability defect: the measure can never
  reach its own theoretical maximum), while using the reduced
  (positive-count) `n` makes "how many names happened to hedge this month"
  enter the max-entropy benchmark itself, entangling "how many risk
  generators are there" with "how concentrated is risk among them" — two
  different questions.
- **(a) is the simplest: no renormalization step, `n` stays fixed at the
  actual subset size** (direct comparability with `entropy_concentration`'s
  own convention, which never renormalizes either), and its interpretation
  ("a name generating no net risk contributes nothing to the concentration
  of risk-generating capacity") is simple to state and defend.

**Known limitation, flagged rather than resolved:** the ~15% relative
spread in the most recent months (2026-04 through 2026-07 — exactly the
period with the most negative contributions) means `risk_entropy`'s exact
level in this specific window is more convention-sensitive than elsewhere
in the sample. **Planned Phase-6 robustness check, not a reason to change
the convention now:** recompute `risk_entropy` under convention (c) and
confirm no downstream Phase 4/5 result that uses it changes qualitatively.

## Point-in-time entrant handling

`risk_share_top{k}` occasionally undercounts a cohort member that is
excluded from Sigma_t for insufficient trailing history (most notably
Tesla, permno 93436, for 11 consecutive months in 2021 following its
2020-12 S&P 500 entry — see the estimation note) — logged as "topk-cohort
attrition" in the build log, not silently absorbed. `n_subset_included`
(95-100 of nominal 100) tracks how many names actually entered Sigma_t
each month.

## Frequency, source, output

- **Frequency:** monthly, month-end — same convention as
  `concentration_measures_monthly` and the CSI.
- **Source:** `data_final/universe/` (daily constituent panel), via
  `src/concentration/covariance_estimation.py`.
- **Computed in:** `src/concentration/build_risk_concentration_panel.py`
  (orchestration) + `src/concentration/risk_measures.py` (pure per-month
  math on `(weights, Sigma_t)`).
- **Output:**
  `data_final/concentration/risk_concentration_measures_monthly_<date>.csv`
  — columns `date, n_subset_nominal, n_subset_included,
  n_excluded_insufficient_history, shrinkage_intensity, n_eff_risk,
  risk_entropy, n_negative_contributions, risk_share_top5, risk_share_top7,
  risk_share_top10`.
- **Burn-in:** 252-trading-day rolling window → first defined observation
  **2000-12-29** (308 of 319 possible months defined; all 19 GFC months,
  2007-12 to 2009-06, covered — see the estimation note for the full
  check).
- **Construction parameters logged to:**
  `outputs/logs/build_risk_concentration_panel_<date>.txt`.

## Sanity checks (2026-08-24)

`outputs/figures/risk_concentration_sanity_check_20260824.png` — small
multiples over 2000-2026 with the same GFC/COVID/Mag7-AI event shading
used in the capital-concentration sanity check.

- **Secular pattern parallels capital concentration, at a higher level:**
  `risk_share_top10` runs from ~0.35-0.40 (2000-2010) down to a trough of
  **0.241 (2010-12)**, then rises to a peak of **0.735 (2024-12)** before
  pulling back to ~0.62-0.67 in 2026 — versus capital `cr_10`'s 24.8% →
  37.0% range over a similar span (`docs/variable_definitions/cr_k.md`).
  Mega-cap names generate risk disproportionately to their capital weight
  (see the collinearity check's internal test below for why this is
  expected even under no correlation), so a higher level here than in
  `cr_10` is expected, not a red flag.
- **`n_eff_risk` mirrors this inversely:** from a peak of **67.3 (2014-04)**
  down to a trough of **10.5 (2024-10)** — a >6x drop in the "equivalent
  number of equal-risk-contributing names" over the sample.
- **Shrinkage intensity spikes at every expected stress episode** (COVID
  2020-03 = 1.000, GFC/Lehman 2008-10 = 0.588, US-downgrade/European
  sovereign stress 2011-08 = 0.563) and falls to its lowest, most stable
  range (~0.10-0.15) during the 2022-2026 Mag7/AI period — see the
  estimation note for interpretation. This is itself a validation that the
  estimator responds to genuine regime shifts rather than producing a
  flat or noisy shrinkage series.
- **A nuance worth flagging, not resolving:** during the COVID window
  itself (2020-02 to 2020-04), `risk_share_top10` briefly **dips** (from
  ~0.40 to ~0.35) even as `shrinkage_intensity` spikes to its sample
  maximum — i.e., the point estimate of concentration and the estimation-
  noise diagnostic moved in different directions at the same moment. This
  is plausible (a broad-based crash can temporarily reduce the *relative*
  dominance of the largest names even while making the covariance harder
  to estimate) but is flagged here rather than explained away, since it is
  exactly the kind of thing a Phase-6 window-length robustness check
  should look at again.
- **`n_subset_included` stays in [95, 100]** across the full sample (median
  99) — the complete-case exclusion is a minor correction, not a
  structural gap.

## Collinearity check (2026-08-24)

`src/concentration/risk_concentration_collinearity_check.py`, run on 296
overlapping monthly observations (2001-12 to 2026-07 — the intersection of
the risk-concentration panel's 2000-12 start and the CSI's own 2001-12
start after its 24-month burn-in). Same threshold and dual-basis
methodology as the Phase-2/Phase-3 check
(`csi_construction.md`/`collinearity_check.py`): |r| > 0.90, Pearson OR
Spearman, on both **levels** and **first differences (MoM change)**. Full
tables in `outputs/tables/risk_concentration_*_correlation_*_20260824.csv`,
heatmaps in `outputs/figures/risk_concentration_*collinearity_20260824.png`.

### Internal (the five risk-concentration candidates against each other)

**Levels:** every pair exceeds 0.90 (Pearson 0.94-0.99, Spearman
0.92-0.99) — expected, all five candidates share the same secular
concentration trend documented for capital concentration.

**First differences:** no pair reaches 0.90.
- `risk_share_top5`/`top7`/`top10` correlate with each other at only
  **0.52-0.67** (Pearson) on differences — notably lower mutual
  correlation than capital concentration's `cr_5`/`cr_7`/`cr_10` family
  achieved on differences (0.87-0.95) — i.e., the choice of `k` matters
  *more* for risk-space top-k cohorts than it did for capital weight
  cohorts. No single `k` is redundant with another by this project's own
  0.90 threshold.
- `n_eff_risk` (sign-flipped) and `risk_entropy` are the most mutually
  correlated internal pair (Pearson 0.743, Spearman 0.878) — the closest
  analogue to the `hhi`/`effective_n` relationship in Phase 2, though
  notably weaker (Phase 2's Spearman-on-differences for that pair was
  0.96, close to a deterministic identity; here it is 0.878, meaningfully
  below deterministic). Not treated as a mathematical identity — `n_eff_risk`
  and `risk_entropy` are related but not literally reparametrizations of
  each other the way `hhi`/`effective_n` are.

### Cross-dimension (risk-concentration candidates vs. `hhi`, `cr_10`, `entropy_concentration`, CSI)

**Levels:** every risk-concentration candidate correlates with every
capital-concentration/CSI series at 0.90-0.95 — again, shared secular
trend, not yet informative on its own (this is exactly why this project's
own collinearity protocol insists on the differenced basis before drawing
conclusions).

**First differences: no pair reaches 0.90 — the maximum across all 20
risk-x-capital pairs is 0.537** (`risk_entropy` vs. `hhi`, Spearman;
0.504 Pearson). Every `risk_share_top{k}` vs. its same-k `cr_k` comparison
(the specific pair `csi_construction.md` flagged as "the pair the paper's
own Section 4 argument predicts may still be highly correlated in
practice") comes in well below threshold too: `risk_share_top10` vs.
`cr_10` = 0.369 (Pearson), 0.382 (Spearman).

**Reading:** on genuine short-run (month-over-month) comovement, risk
concentration is clearly and consistently distinct from capital
concentration — no cross-dimension pair comes anywhere near the 0.90
redundancy threshold, and the closest pairs (`risk_entropy` vs. `hhi`/
`cr_10`, ~0.43-0.54) still leave the large majority of variance
unexplained by the capital-concentration side. This is a materially
cleaner separation than any of the internal Phase-2 capital-concentration
pairs achieved against each other (where the tightest surviving pairs
after differencing were still 0.87-0.94). **Risk concentration adds
information distinct from capital concentration, not a transformation of
it**, at least at this window/subset/shrinkage specification.
